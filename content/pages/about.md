## What this is

Gambling Research is an index of new peer-reviewed studies and preprints about gambling in all its forms: sports betting, online casinos and slots, lotteries, loot boxes, prediction markets and the trading that behaves like gambling. Every night it collects what PubMed, OpenAlex, Crossref and the journals' own feeds have added, removes duplicates, tags each study by topic, design and country, and lists it with a link to the publisher's record. Beside the index sits a feed of policy, legal and public-health news, and a short weekly digest of the studies worth reading.

The reference pages are the things people in this field keep looking up: [where to publish](/journals/), [the conference calendar](/conferences/), [public datasets](/datasets/), [screening instruments](/measures/) and [how to do research without a university behind you](/guide/).

## Who edits it

Chris Beerman, LCSW, CADC, a licensed clinical social worker in Chicago who treats gambling disorder in private practice and publishes research on sports betting, prediction markets and self-exclusion. ORCID [0009-0004-8277-2086](https://orcid.org/0009-0004-8277-2086).

## Independence

This site takes no funding, advertising, data or affiliate income from gambling operators, suppliers, affiliates, lotteries, prediction-market exchanges or bodies they fund. It carries no advertising of any kind and no tracking beyond what the hosting provider logs. Studies appear in the index because they match the search rules on the [methodology page](/methodology/), and in the digest because the editor judged them worth reading, never because anyone asked.

Interests to declare: the editor runs a private practice that treats gambling disorder and may benefit reputationally from this work; he maintains a free, non-commercial educational website about stopping sports betting; he is an author of some of the studies the index lists, which are treated exactly like every other entry. He has no relationship with any wagering operator, exchange, intermediary or exclusion service.

## How it is made

The index is software: a nightly job that queries public bibliographic databases and feeds, with its rules published in the [repository](https://github.com/Chris-Beerman/gambling-research.com). The digest and the reference pages are written by the editor with AI assistance for research and drafting, and checked by him before publication. Errors are his.

## Corrections and contact

A misfiled study, a wrong date, a missing journal: write to <editor@gambling-research.com> or open an issue on GitHub. Corrections are made in the data, not quietly on the page, so they carry forward.

## Citing the index

The index data is released under CC0 at [/data/papers.json](/data/papers.json). Cite the underlying study, not this site; if you need to cite the index itself, "Gambling Research (gambling-research.com), index retrieved [date]" is enough.

## If you need help with gambling

This site is for research. If gambling is causing harm to you or someone close to you, the National Problem Gambling Helpline in the United States is 1-800-GAMBLER (call or text), available around the clock. Outside the US, your national helpline is listed by your gambling regulator or health service.
