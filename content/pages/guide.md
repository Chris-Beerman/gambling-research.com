## What an institution gives you, and the free substitute

| The institution provides | The substitute |
|---|---|
| Library access to journals | [PubMed Central](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov), [OpenAlex](https://openalex.org) and [Unpaywall](https://unpaywall.org) for free copies; preprint servers; emailing the corresponding author, who almost always sends the PDF |
| Database searching (PsycINFO, Scopus) | PubMed, OpenAlex and Semantic Scholar cover most gambling research; for a formal review, a co-author with library access runs the proprietary databases |
| An institutional review board | An independent IRB (see below), a determination that the work is not human-subjects research, or a design that needs no review |
| Statistical software | R, Python, [jamovi](https://www.jamovi.org) and [JASP](https://jasp-stats.org) are free and accepted everywhere |
| A survey platform | Free tiers of the usual platforms, or an open-source platform run on your own server |
| An affiliation line | Your practice or organisation; no journal listed on the [journals page](/journals/) requires an institution |
| Colleagues | A second coder, a co-author with an IRB, a methodologist; recovery organisations, state councils and professional associations are where they are found |

## Ethics review

Three situations, three routes.

**No human participants.** Platform audits, content analyses of marketing, analyses of public aggregate data (search trends, regulator reports), document censuses, reviews and conceptual papers. No review is required; journals accept the statement "This study did not involve human participants; ethics approval was not required." Public transaction-level datasets of pseudonymous accounts usually qualify as not human-subjects research under US regulations (45 CFR 46.102(e)), but a journal editor can still ask for a formal determination, which an independent IRB will issue.

**Consented case studies.** A single consented case report is generally not research under the regulations, and some journals (*Clinical Case Studies*, *Journal of Financial Therapy*) accept consent to publish in place of approval. Others (*Journal of Gambling Studies*, *IJMHA*, *Clinical Social Work Journal*) require ethics approval even for case reports, so target the former. Written, revocable consent; de-identify and say what was changed; let the client read the manuscript.

**Surveys and interviews.** This is human-subjects research and needs review before data collection; retrospective approval is usually impossible. Independent IRBs (Solutions IRB, Pearl IRB, WCG, Advarra and others) review exempt and expedited protocols for roughly $1,000 to $2,000 in two to four weeks; verify current fees. A university co-author brings their IRB, library access and, at the higher-tier journals, better odds. Recruit through recovery communities, blocking-software user groups, treatment providers and state councils, never your own current clients.

## Preregister, then deposit

The [Open Science Framework](https://osf.io) is free, needs no affiliation, and timestamps a registration permanently; embargoes of up to four years are available if a plan should stay private during review. *Addiction* treats unregistered analyses as exploratory and other journals are following. After the study, deposit de-identified data, code and codebooks in the same project for a DOI; explain why transcripts or clinical data are not shared.

## Identity and record

Register an [ORCID](https://orcid.org) before the first submission and use one author name everywhere. Keep a public list of your papers (OSF or your own site); reviewers and conference committees look.

## Preprints

PsyArXiv and OSF Preprints (behavioural science), SSRN (law, economics), medRxiv (health) and Research Square are free and establish priority, which matters in fast-moving areas such as prediction markets. The journals on the [journals page](/journals/) permit prior preprints unless marked otherwise.

## Writing to a budget

Word limits at the main gambling journals run from 2,000 to 6,500 words; the [journals page](/journals/) lists them. Reporting checklists (STROBE, PRISMA, COREQ, CARE, from the [EQUATOR network](https://www.equator-network.org)) are free and reviewers expect them. Content analyses need a codebook, dual coding on a subsample and an agreement statistic.

## Disclosure

Declare the practice, the product, the website or the consultancy that touches the subject of the paper, in every paper, in one standard sentence. A non-commercial interest is still declared. Omitting it is the one mistake that cannot be fixed later.

## Conferences

Practitioner conferences accept clinician-led work readily and are where collaborators are met; the [conference calendar](/conferences/) has deadlines. Preliminary data from a registered study beats a promised study.

## A realistic sequence

1. A no-review study (audit, content analysis, public data) that can be finished in three months, preregistered on OSF.
2. A practice or clinical paper (a treatment component, a consented case) for a clinical journal.
3. The survey or interview study, with an independent IRB or a university co-author, preregistered, deposited.

Each one makes the next easier: a publication record, an OSF profile, a co-author, and a reason for a journal to take the fourth paper seriously.
