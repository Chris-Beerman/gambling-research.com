Licensing notes are from the authors' own statements and the literature; confirm before building a study or a clinical tool on an instrument. "Free with citation" means the authors have said so publicly.

## Problem-gambling severity and screening

| Instrument | Authors | Items and timeframe | What it gives | Licence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Problem Gambling Severity Index (PGSI) | Ferris and Wynne, 2001 (from the Canadian Problem Gambling Index) | 9 items; past 12 months; scored 0 to 27 | Four bands: non-problem (0), low risk (1 to 2), moderate risk (3 to 7), problem gambling (8 or more). The standard population and treatment measure worldwide | Free with citation to Ferris and Wynne (2001) and no charge to users |
| Brief Problem Gambling Screen (BPGS) | Volberg and Williams, 2012 | 5 items | Screen derived from the PPGM for surveys | Free with citation |
| Problem and Pathological Gambling Measure (PPGM) | Williams and Volberg, 2010 | 14 items | Classification that requires both problems and impaired control; used in Canadian and Massachusetts surveys | Free with citation |
| South Oaks Gambling Screen (SOGS) | Lesieur and Blume, 1987 | 20 items; lifetime or past year | The older standard; over-identifies in general populations | Free |
| NODS and NODS-CLiP | Gerstein and colleagues, 1999; Toce-Gerstein, Gerstein and Volberg, 2009 | 17 items; CLiP is 3 items (control, lying, preoccupation) | DSM-IV-based; CLiP is a fast clinical screen | Free |
| Brief Biosocial Gambling Screen (BBGS) | Gebauer, LaBrie and Shaffer, 2010 | 3 items; past 12 months | Withdrawal, lying, financial bailout; derived from NESARC | Free |
| Lie/Bet Questionnaire | Johnson and colleagues, 1997 | 2 items | The shortest screen; useful in primary care | Free |
| DSM-5 gambling disorder criteria | American Psychiatric Association, 2013 | 9 criteria; past 12 months | Four or more = gambling disorder; mild (4 to 5), moderate (6 to 7), severe (8 to 9). Structured interviews exist (for example the GDIT) | Criteria are copyrighted text; clinical use is standard |
| Gambling Disorder Identification Test (GDIT) | Molander and colleagues, 2021 | 9 items | Consumption, symptoms and negative consequences, modelled on the AUDIT | Free with citation |
| Victorian Gambling Screen | Ben-Tovim and colleagues, 2001 | 21 items | Harm-to-self subscale is the usual scoring | Free |

## Harm

| Instrument | Authors | Items | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Short Gambling Harm Screen (SGHS) | Browne and colleagues, 2018 | 10 items; past 12 months | Counts harms rather than symptoms; widely used in Australia and increasingly elsewhere |
| Gambling Harms Scale (72-item inventory and short forms) | Browne, Langham and colleagues, 2016 onward | 72 items across financial, relationship, emotional, health, work and other domains | The source list from which the SGHS was drawn; affected-other versions exist |

## Cognitions, motives and urges

| Instrument | Authors | Items | What it measures |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gambling Related Cognitions Scale (GRCS) | Raylu and Oei, 2004 | 23 items | Illusion of control, predictive control, interpretive bias, gambling expectancies, inability to stop |
| Gamblers' Beliefs Questionnaire (GBQ) | Steenbergh and colleagues, 2002 | 21 items | Luck and perseverance, illusion of control |
| Gambling Motives Questionnaire (GMQ) and GMQ-Financial | Stewart and Zack, 2008; Dechant, 2014 | 15 items (GMQ-F adds financial motives) | Enhancement, coping, social and financial motives |
| Gambling Urge Scale (GUS) | Raylu and Oei, 2004 | 6 items | Current urge; sensitive to change in treatment |
| Gambling Symptom Assessment Scale (G-SAS) | Kim, Grant and colleagues, 2009 | 12 items; past week | Trial outcome measure for urges, thoughts and behaviour |
| Gambling Self-Efficacy Questionnaire (GSEQ) | May and colleagues, 2003 | 16 items | Confidence to resist gambling in high-risk situations |
| Positive Play Scale | Wood, Wohl, Tabri and Philander, 2017 | 14 items | Responsible-gambling beliefs and behaviours |

## Newer domains

| Instrument | Authors | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Trading Disorder Scale | Coloma-Carmona and colleagues, 2025 (*Journal of Behavioral Addictions*) | First validated measure of gambling-like trading; pairs with the PGSI, which asks about gambling rather than trading |
| Risky Loot Box Index (RLI) | Brooks and Clark, 2019 | Loot-box purchasing and its links to problem gambling |
| Problem Gambling Severity Index adapted for sports betting | Various | No consensus adaptation exists; most studies use the PGSI unchanged and add product-specific items |
| Prediction-market and event-contract measures | None validated as of 2026 | A gap; the PGSI with product items is the current practice |

## Affected others

Instruments for partners and family members are fewer. Affected-other versions of the gambling harm checklists exist (Browne, Rockloff and colleagues); the PGSI has been administered as a "concerned significant other" report in some surveys; and family-impact measures from the alcohol field have been adapted. Expect to justify the choice.

## Using an instrument in a public tool

Posting a scored screen on a website is clinical publishing. Three things keep it defensible: use the instrument's exact items and scoring, cite the authors as they ask, and present results as a screen rather than a diagnosis ("the score is in the range associated with problem gambling", never "you have gambling disorder"). The PGSI's authors ask that it stay free of charge wherever it is used.
