Four things decide most submissions.

**Fees are rarely the barrier.** Almost every relevant journal is hybrid: the subscription route costs nothing, and the article-processing charge (roughly $2,500 to $5,000) applies only if you choose open access. Two good journals are diamond open access, free to publish and free to read: *Critical Gambling Studies* and the *Journal of Financial Therapy*. The exceptions are fully open-access titles with no free route: the *Journal of Behavioral Addictions* (about €1,400, and the highest-impact journal in the field), *Addictive Behaviors Reports*, and the JMIR, Frontiers and BMC families. The *Journal of Gambling Issues*, historically free, became a hybrid journal on July 1, 2026 (APC CAD $2,500); its fee page describes a subscription route without stating that it is free, so confirm with the editor before submitting.

**Ethics approval is the real gate.** The Springer titles (*Journal of Gambling Studies*, *International Journal of Mental Health and Addiction*, *Clinical Social Work Journal*), *Journal of Gambling Issues*, *Qualitative Health Research* and *Cognitive and Behavioral Practice* require an ethics-approval statement for anything involving human participants or their data, and *Journal of Gambling Studies* states that case reports need approval and that retrospective approval usually cannot be obtained. Studies with no human participants (platform audits, content analyses, public search data, reviews, conceptual papers) avoid the gate. Consented single-case studies are safest at *Clinical Case Studies*, which accepts a "not applicable" statement plus consent to publish, and at the *Journal of Financial Therapy*, which states no IRB policy. See the [guide for unaffiliated researchers](/guide/) on getting review without a university.

**Word budgets are tight.** *Journal of Gambling Studies* caps original research and reviews at 4,000 words; *Addictive Behaviors* at 3,500 (short communications 2,000); *Addiction* at 3,500 for observational reports and 4,500 for mixed methods; *IJMHA* allows 6,000; *Journal of Gambling Issues* 6,500 (reviews 8,000); *Critical Gambling Studies* 5,000 to 8,000.

**Disclose gambling-related interests.** Every journal below requires a competing-interests statement, and several (*Addiction*, the Springer titles, *Critical Gambling Studies*) ask specifically about gambling-industry ties within the past three years. Undisclosed interests are grounds for correction or retraction; disclose and move on.

## Requirements that apply almost everywhere

**Author identity.** Register an [ORCID](https://orcid.org) (free; mandatory at *Addiction* and *Clinical Case Studies*, expected everywhere) and use one author name consistently. No journal here requires an institutional affiliation or email; a practice or an independent affiliation is accepted.

**Ethics statement wording by design.** No human participants: "This study did not involve human participants; ethics approval was not required." Consented case study: "The client provided written informed consent for publication; identifying details have been altered." Survey or interviews: "Approved by [IRB name], protocol [number]; all participants provided informed consent." Composite vignettes must be labelled as composites.

**Reporting guidelines.** STROBE for cross-sectional surveys; COREQ or SRQR for interviews; CARE for case reports; PRISMA or PRISMA-ScR for reviews. Content analyses have no formal checklist, so reviewers look for a codebook, dual coding on a subsample and an agreement statistic.

**Open science.** A data-availability statement is mandatory at *Journal of Gambling Studies*, *Addictive Behaviors* (deposit required), *Clinical Case Studies* and *Addiction*. The [Open Science Framework](https://osf.io) is free for de-identified data, code, codebooks and preregistrations. *Addiction* treats unregistered analyses as exploratory.

**Disclosures.** Competing interests, funding ("no external funding" is a complete answer) and generative-AI use, now required at *Critical Gambling Studies*, *Journal of Financial Therapy*, *Addictive Behaviors* and *Qualitative Health Research*.

**Mechanics.** Double-anonymized journals need a blinded manuscript with a separate title page and third-person self-citation. APA 7 references at the Springer, SAGE, CDS Press, CGS and JFT titles; Vancouver-style numbered references at *Addiction* and the *Journal of Addiction Medicine*. Most journals ask for three to five suggested reviewers. Preprints (PsyArXiv, OSF, SSRN) are permitted by the Springer, Elsevier, SAGE and Wiley titles listed here.

**Turnaround.** Median first decisions are days at Springer and Elsevier (desk rejections included) and one to two months for full review; expect three to six months to acceptance and acceptance rates of 20 to 30 percent at mid-tier titles. Plan a cascade (primary, backup, then a diamond or lower-tier title) so a rejection costs a week, not a season.
