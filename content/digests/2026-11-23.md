A randomised experiment in Wuhan finds that harm-focused warning messages cut lottery chasing where benefit-focused ones do not, and two population surveys carry the rest of the week: a Spanish household study in which gambling disorder is present in about one in eight households that have had a suicide or attempt, and a British analysis of how much regular gamblers spend and how often they play before PGSI scores climb. In the courts, the Fourth Circuit became the third appeals court to side with a state against Kalshi, a New York judge sent the attorney general's suit back to state court, and the Senate passed its first prediction-market bill.

## Studies worth reading

### [Designing gambling-harm prevention messages: A randomized experiment on lottery-chasing persistence in China](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pmedr.2026.103715)

*Preventive Medicine Reports, China.*

Between January and March 2024, 160 university students in Wuhan who had bought sports-lottery products at least monthly for three months were randomised across the eight cells of a 2 by 2 by 2 design: question-based self-appraisal or declarative messages, benefit-focused or harm-focused content, and a rigged lottery task on a net-loss or net-win trajectory; 157 were analysed. After 30 compulsory trials, participants could play up to 30 more, with a message shown after every trial. Harm-focused messages produced fewer optional trials than benefit-focused ones (7.5 against 13.3, a model-estimated difference of 5.8, confidence interval 2.7 to 8.9), and an exploratory three-way interaction suggested declarative harm messages worked best after losses and question-based ones after wins. There was no no-message control, 72 percent of the sample were women, the stakes were a few yuan, the author calls the interaction underpowered, and several benefit-focused messages invoking public welfare are the state lottery's own. Funded by the National Social Science Fund of China; no competing interests. Free full text.

### [Gambling Disorder, Family Context, and Suicide Risk: Evidence From a Spanish Household Survey](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10899-026-10532-0)

*Journal of Gambling Studies, Spain.*

Economists at the University of Zaragoza used the FOESSA 2021 survey, a nationally representative in-person household survey of Spain with 18,264 observations, in which one respondent answers for the household. Gambling disorder was coded present if any member had had gambling problems, diagnosed or not, and suicidal behaviour if any member had attempted or died by suicide. Gambling disorder was present in about 13 percent of households with suicidal behaviour against 0.3 percent of those without, and difficulty making ends meet in 84 percent against 59 percent; a structural equation model put most of the association on the direct path, with a smaller indirect path through financial strain. One person reports for everyone, attempts and deaths are pooled and the design is cross-sectional, so this is co-occurrence, not sequence. Open access; no funding reported.

### [Exploring the relationship between gambling consumption and problem gambling severity index scores: An analysis of two cross-sectional datasets of people in Britain who gamble regularly](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.addbeh.2026.108917)

*Addictive Behaviors, United Kingdom.*

Sarah Tipping and Heather Wardle take the Lower-Risk Gambling Guidelines as a frame and ask where, in British data, consumption starts to carry harm. The Regular Gamblers Survey, a random probability survey of 1,320 adults who had gambled in the past four weeks on more than the lottery, was paired with an equivalent sub-sample of 4,268 from the Gambling Survey for Great Britain. PGSI scores rose linearly with net spend as a share of household income and with frequency, and in a J shape with the number of activities; the inflexion points fell at more than 4 percent of household income, more than five activities in four weeks and more than four gambling days a month. The data are cross-sectional and the thresholds are statistical cut-points, not tested against later harm, but they are the first British numbers I have seen that a bank, operator or clinician could use as a flag. Funded by Gambling Research Exchange Ontario; the accepted manuscript is free from the University of Glasgow repository.

### [Are Prediction Platform Bettors Distinct from Sportsbook Bettors? Evidence from Skill Perceptions and Positive Play Attitudes](https://doi.org/10.9741/2327-8455.1529)

*UNLV Gaming Research & Review Journal, United States.*

Two Prolific samples of US adults, 523 sportsbook bettors and 503 sports event contract traders, compared on perceived skill, perceived luck and the four Positive Play dimensions. Prediction-market users reported more skill and less luck, and scored lower on gambling literacy, honesty and control, and personal responsibility; the abstract reports no result for pre-commitment, and its author, Brandon Mastromartino of San Diego State, reads the pattern as a misalignment between perceived control and responsible play. Online panels and self-report limit it, and I have read only the abstract, as the PDF would not open. Industry-funded: supported by the American Gaming Association, which the paper says had no involvement in the study.

### ['Not on GamStop': Mapping unlicensed gambling websites' user journeys from social media advertisements to unsuccessful withdrawals](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.puhe.2026.106559)

*Public Health, United Kingdom.*

Researchers at Swansea and Bristol took ten "not on GamStop" adverts recorded in the Meta Ad Library between July and December 2025, which together reached 42,718 UK users, 69 percent of them men, and followed each to its operator. One researcher registered with GamStop under genuine details, then opened an account at every site with the same details. None of the ten held a British licence, recognised the GamStop registration, verified identity at sign-up or offered deposit or loss limits; an email disclosing loss of control brought account closure without support; eight of nine completed deposits carried inaccurate merchant category codes on the bank statement; and every withdrawal attempt failed, mostly on identity checks demanded only at cash-out. Ten higher-traffic operators from one ad platform at one moment is a purposive sample, and the terms were read as written, not as enforced. The payment-code finding is the actionable one, since a bank's gambling block cannot see these deposits. No funding; the authors declare no conflicts, listing past grants from gambling-research funders including GREO and the International Center for Responsible Gambling. Free full text.

## Policy and public health news

- [Kalshiex LLC v. Martin](https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/11066946/kalshiex-llc-v-john-martin/) (Fourth Circuit, November 18): the court affirmed, 2 to 1, the denial of Kalshi's preliminary injunction against Maryland; per [InGame](https://cdcgaming.com/brief/fourth-circuit-panel-sides-with-maryland-over-kalshi-but-the-three-judges-differ-on-key-questions/), Judge Gregory found no preemption of state gambling law, Judge Thacker found the contracts are not swaps, and Judge Benjamin dissented on both.
- [People of the State of New York v. KalshiEX LLC](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/73700030/people-of-the-state-of-new-york-by-letitia-james-attorney-general-of-the-state-of-new-york-v-kalshiex-llc/) (S.D.N.Y., November 19): opinion and order on the attorney general's motion to remand; [ABC News reports](https://cdcgaming.com/brief/trump-administration-tries-to-intervene-after-kalshi-is-threatened-with-restraining-order/) that Judge Broderick returned the case to state court, that Judge Schofield denied the CFTC's emergency bid to block a state-court restraining order, and that the state hearing was set for Monday.
- [S. 4035, DEATH BETS Act](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/4035) United States, prohibits CFTC-registered entities from listing contracts on terrorism, assassination, war, US military operations or an individual's death, passed the Senate on November 19; the sponsors' [release](https://www.schiff.senate.gov/news/press-releases/news-senate-unanimously-passes-sens-schiff-curtis-legislation-to-explicitly-ban-death-and-war-prediction-contracts/) says it passed unanimously, with an amendment that it does not affect the sports-contract litigation.
- [Major casino operator severs ties with problem gambling group over Kalshi membership](https://cdcgaming.com/brief/major-casino-operator-severs-ties-with-problem-gambling-group-over-kalshi-membership/) MGM Resorts and BetMGM cancelled their National Council on Problem Gambling memberships by letter on November 18 over the council's admission of Kalshi, the week Kalshi and Novig [wrote to 35 states](https://www.thesportsgeek.com/blog/kalshi-novig-pitch-selfexclude-io-35-states-court-fight/) proposing SelfExclude.io, a voluntary exclusion list for prediction markets.
- [Illinois offers 'fragmented' response to gambling addiction, internal state report says](https://capitolnewsillinois.com/news/illinois-offers-fragmented-response-to-gambling-addiction-internal-state-report-says/) A report presented to the governor in August: new self-exclusion enrolments rose only from 350 to 595 a year since 2022, slot machines sit outside the programme, Medicaid does not cover gambling disorder as a distinct condition, and under 0.1 percent of state gambling revenue goes to treatment.
- [UK rejects Lords' call for gambling ad ban but agrees to widen ministers' online powers](https://igamingbusiness.com/legal-compliance/uk-rejects-lords-call-for-gambling-ad-ban-but-agrees-to-widen-ministers-online-powers/) The government turns down a comprehensive advertising ban, a statutory ad regulator and limits on ads to under-25s, but agrees to seek to close the gap in section 333 of the Gambling Act that limits ministers' powers over online ads.
- [Ashton suicide ruling narrows route to negligence claims against gambling operators, says legal expert](https://igamingbusiness.com/legal-compliance/ashton-negligence-claims-against-gambling-operators-says-lawyer/) The High Court dismissed Ann-Marie Ashton's negligence claim against Betfair's operator on November 13 for insufficient evidence that the operator knew or should have known of a gambling disorder, while holding that a duty of care can arise where the risk is foreseeable.
