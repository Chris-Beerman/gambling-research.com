A quieter week for studies and a loud one for courts. The paper I would hand to a clinic is a US cohort of 1,518 people entering gambling treatment, in which the 31 percent who endorsed suicidal thoughts at intake did no worse on attendance or symptom change; the policy news is Louisiana's restraining order against every prediction-market brand it could name, and the federal suits filed in reply.

## Studies worth reading

### [Suicidal Ideation Endorsed on the Patient Health Questionnaire-9 at Intake and Treatment Outcomes in Gambling Disorder Care: Retrospective Cohort Study](https://doi.org/10.2196/106739)

*JMIR Formative Research, United States.*

De-identified records from Birches Health, a US telehealth provider for gambling and other behavioural addictions, covering 1,518 adults who entered gambling disorder care, attended at least one session and completed a PHQ-9 at intake. Suicidal ideation, any score above zero on item 9, was endorsed by 465 patients (30.6 percent); they had higher depression scores (PHQ-8 mean 15.2 against 8.7) and gambling severity (G-SAS 22.7 against 19.1) but did not differ by age, gender or other diagnoses. After adjustment, ideation did not predict appointments attended (incidence rate ratio 1.05, confidence interval 0.92 to 1.19) and did not change the rate at which gambling severity fell, which it did across the whole sample. The data are one provider's own caseload, with no comparison group and no study period in the abstract, and I have read the accepted preprint rather than the final article; one author works for Birches Health and three for Fit Minded, a research consultancy. The practical point stands: a positive item 9 is a reason to assess risk, not to expect dropout.

### [Identifying Significant Correlates of Adolescent Online Gambling Using A Random Forest Approach: A Cross-Sectional Study](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10899-026-10547-7)

*Journal of Gambling Studies, Canada.*

Three years of the COMPASS school survey, 2017-18 to 2019-20, from 151 secondary schools in four provinces, 103,008 students in all; between 3.5 and 3.8 percent reported online gambling in the past 30 days each year. Across 25 random-forest runs the factors in the top ten every time were a flourishing score, physical activity, symptoms of depression and anxiety, emotional dysregulation, sleep and daily screen time; video gaming made the top ten in 56 percent of runs and gender in 16 percent. The random forest did not beat ordinary logistic regression (AUROC 0.821 against 0.836), the outcome is a single yes-or-no item, and the data predate Ontario's 2022 online market. For a school or paediatric service, the teenager gambling online looks first like a teenager in poor mental health. Funded by the Canadian Institutes of Health Research; free full text.

### [Why do Adolescents Engage in Gambling Chasing? A Multidimensional Model of Motivational, Cognitive, and Disinhibitory Processes](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11121-026-02009-8)

*Prevention Science, Italy.*

A laboratory experiment with 258 adolescents aged 16 to 19, randomly told after 30 trials of a computerised chasing task that their money was preserved or lost, then free to stop or play up to 30 more. The induced loss made no significant difference to whether they continued or how long they persisted. What did predict continuing was craving, measured as anticipation and desire on the GACS, and steeper delay discounting; persistence was also linked to alcohol use, and gambling severity mattered only indirectly through those three. The stakes were small and the trait measures cross-sectional, so the paper identifies candidate processes rather than causes, but it cuts against the standard account of chasing in teenagers as a reaction to losing. No external funding.

### [Incomplete compliance: Loot box prevalence, presence disclosures, probability disclosures, and Compu Gacha policy in Japanese mobile games](https://doi.org/10.1556/2006.2026.00139)

*Journal of Behavioral Addictions, Japan.*

Japan has required probability disclosure for loot boxes and banned the "Compu Gacha" set-completion mechanic since 2012, mostly through industry self-regulation. In late 2025 the authors played the first hour of the 100 highest-grossing iPhone games in Japan. Ninety-one contained loot boxes; of those, 17.6 percent did not disclose them on the Apple App Store and 33.0 percent did not on Google Play, 90.1 percent gave probabilities for at least one loot box but only 41.8 percent for every one encountered, and four carried suspected Compu Gacha. One hour of play will miss later mechanics, and "suspected" is the authors' word. The lesson from the oldest loot box regime is that a disclosure rule without enforcement is complied with in part. Free full text.

### [Prediction markets in the United States: a youth public health perspective](https://doi.org/10.3389/fradm.2026.1976366)

*Frontiers in Adolescent Medicine, United States.*

A perspective by Tammy Chung of Rutgers, not a study. It argues that prediction markets are available in all 50 states, to 18-year-olds on some platforms, with gamified features and marketing that resemble betting apps, and that almost nothing is known about youth prevalence or exposure. The useful parts are a proposed adaptation of the two-item Lie-Bet screen for prediction-market trading, untested there, and the figure that only 15 percent of young adults recall a primary care provider asking about gambling. Every number is borrowed from other surveys or an industry report, and the author discloses that generative AI helped assemble the reference list, so check citations before reusing them. Free full text.

## Policy and public health news

- [Louisiana Judge Issues TRO to 31 Prediction Market Entities](https://www.legalsportsreport.com/287011/louisiana-judge-blocks-kalshi-polymarket-29-other-prediction-market-entities/) Judge Jeffrey Johnson of the 21st Judicial District Court signed an ex parte temporary restraining order on November 12 against 31 entities under 13 brands, including Kalshi, Polymarket, Robinhood and Coinbase, barring event contracts on sports, elections and awards in Louisiana without a state licence; it expires November 22, with a preliminary injunction hearing set for November 30. [Louisiana State v. Kalshi Inc.](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/75119504/louisiana-state-v-kalshi-inc/) (E.D. La., November 13): notice of removal to federal court, followed on November 14 by a filing to vacate.
- [CFTC Files Lawsuit Against Arkansas as the Agency Defends its Federal Jurisdiction Over Prediction Markets](https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/9325-26) Filed November 13 to stop the state-court suit Arkansas brought against Kalshi on November 4; Arkansas is the eleventh state the commission has sued, and a [second release the same day](https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases) announces a suit against Louisiana over the order above.
- [KalshiEX LLC v. Williams](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/73601450/kalshiex-llc-v-williams/) (Second Circuit, November 6 and 9): amicus briefs filed by 37 states, the District of Columbia and the Northern Mariana Islands, by the North American and International associations of gaming regulators, by former CFTC chair Gary Gensler, and by the Indian Gaming Association and the National Congress of American Indians.
- [Colorado Targets Athlete Harassment With Exclusion Rule](https://www.legalsportsreport.com/286836/colorado-regulators-target-harassment-insider-betting-with-exclusion-rule/) The Division of Gaming proposes adding people who threaten athletes, coaches or team staff, and anyone who bets in breach of the state's insider betting law, to the involuntary exclusion list; the rule has not been filed yet, and sportsbooks cannot be required to honour the list.
- [Young People and Gambling 2026](https://www.gamblingcommission.gov.uk/statistics-and-research/publication/young-people-and-gambling-2026-official-statistics) The Gambling Commission's survey of 3,867 pupils aged 11 to 17, fielded January to June: 26 percent had spent their own money on gambling in the past year (31 percent in 2022, on a narrower sample), 0.9 percent screened as problem gamblers, 89 percent recalled gambling advertising and 22 percent had bought loot boxes.
- [OHID boycott of Safer Gambling Week sparks industry divide](https://next.io/news/responsible-gambling/ohid-safer-gambling-week-2026-boycott/) In emails first reported by EGR, the Office for Health Improvement and Disparities told the charities it funds through the levy not to back Safer Gambling Week, November 16 to 22, as industry-led and built on individual responsibility; the Gambling Commission says it will support the week.
- [Pa. gambling self-exclusions spread far from casinos](https://www.publicsource.org/pennsylvania-gambling-self-exclusion-data/) About 53,000 people are on Pennsylvania's self-exclusion lists, 46,000 of them residents; Blair County, with no casino, enrolled six people in the decade before online casinos launched in 2019 and 115 last year, the state's third-highest rate.
- [Michigan Gaming Control Board Sets the National Pace Against Illegal Gambling](https://www.michigan.gov/mgcb/news/2026/11/13/mgcb-sets-the-national-pace-against-illegal-gambling) 259 cease-and-desist letters to offshore sites since mid-2024, 166 of them complied with; Robinhood, Coinbase and Polymarket agreed in September and October to stop offering sports contracts to Michigan customers.
