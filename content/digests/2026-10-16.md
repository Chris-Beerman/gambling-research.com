Two of this week's studies use data that gambling research rarely gets: insurance claims for most of the employer-insured United States, and an operator's complete account records. Between them sits an audit that simply phoned every state helpline and recorded what happened.

## Studies worth reading

### [Online Sports Betting Legalization and Behavioral Health Care Use](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2026.3175)

*JAMA Psychiatry, United States.*

A staggered difference-in-differences study of claims from 2016 to 2023, covering almost 62 million enrollee-years of people with employer-sponsored insurance in states that did and did not legalize online sports betting. Legalization was followed by small but measurable increases in antidepressant and anti-anxiety prescription fills and in outpatient visits for depression and anxiety (for men, a 0.57 percentage-point rise in antidepressant use; for women, 1.08 points, with wider confidence intervals). Substance-use treatment did not change, and nothing moved in emergency or inpatient care. The outcomes are care use, not diagnoses of gambling disorder, and the sample excludes the uninsured and publicly insured, so this is a floor rather than a full accounting. It is the strongest population-level evidence so far that legalization carries a behavioral-health cost that health systems absorb.

### [Invisible Obstacles: Audit of Consumer Experience When Calling Gambling Helplines in the United States](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10899-026-10565-5)

*Journal of Gambling Studies, United States.*

Two studies. First, 179 past-year gamblers were asked what they expect from a helpline: a knowledgeable, non-judgmental human rather than an automated system, and most said they would not call back after a bad first experience. Second, trained callers posing as people seeking treatment made 96 audit calls, two per state across 48 states. Ninety-one percent of calls were answered, but only 66 percent of answered calls produced a treatment referral, and on the authors' combined measure of call length, a concrete referral and a helpful experience, 31 percent of calls succeeded. For anyone who puts a helpline number on a website or an intake form, this is the paper to read.

### [Suicidal ideation and psychiatric comorbidity in a nationally representative sample of ED visits with documented gambling concerns](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.genhosppsych.2026.09.017)

*General Hospital Psychiatry, United States.*

From the 2022 Nationwide Emergency Department Sample (111 million weighted adult visits), the authors pulled visits with a gambling disorder code or a "gambling and betting" lifestyle factor, a weighted 1,089 visits. Suicidal ideation was documented in 39.7 percent of them and intentional self-harm injury in 7.8 percent; gambling was rarely the primary diagnosis, which was usually a mood, anxiety or substance-use disorder. The case for routine gambling screening in behavioral-health emergency presentations gets a number. Disclosure: the authors' institution, the Division on Addiction, lists DraftKings and the International Center for Responsible Gaming among its current funders.

### [From scroll to norm: How gambling norms are being shaped by social media sponsorships](https://doi.org/10.1556/2006.2026.00021)

*Journal of Behavioral Addictions, Belgium.*

A 2-by-2 online experiment with 369 young adults aged 18 to 20, varying whether a sponsored post promoted a gambling brand or a control brand and whether it came from an influencer or the brand itself. Exposure to sponsored gambling posts raised participants' estimate of how often a typical student gambles, but the posts were also judged more morally inappropriate, and other norms did not move. Normalization, on this evidence, is about perceived prevalence before it is about approval.

### [Risky parental gambling and child delinquency: longitudinal evidence on financial strain and disrupted parenting](https://doi.org/10.1080/16066359.2026.2741076)

*Addiction Research & Theory, Australia.*

Using 2,993 children in the Longitudinal Study of Australian Children, parental PGSI scores in 2016 predicted child delinquency in 2018 (standardized coefficient 0.18), with part of the association running through financial hardship, reduced parental monitoring and angry parenting. The indirect paths are small, and parental gambling was measured once, but it is one of the few prospective tests of the mechanisms family clinicians assume.

### [Distribution of Losses Among Horse Race Bettors: An Analysis of Account-Based Player Tracking Data](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10899-026-10568-2)

*Journal of Gambling Studies, Norway.*

Norsk Rikstoto, the state horse-betting monopoly, provided account data for every customer from 2021 to 2023 across 13 betting formats. The top 1 percent of bettors accounted for 24.7 percent of all losses and the top 20 percent for 86.0 percent; the Gini coefficient was 0.81. Concentration was higher for single-race products than for multi-race pools. The paper does not say how many of the heaviest losers are harmed, but it does say where the revenue comes from, which is the question regulators keep asking operators.

### [Online vs. In Person Gambling: Does Exposure to Brick-and-Mortar Gambling Shops Lead to Increased Online Gambling?](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10899-026-10566-4)

*Journal of Gambling Studies, Great Britain.*

With a 2022 national survey and a double negative control design meant to handle unmeasured confounding, people in the highest decile of exposure to physical gambling shops had about 1.49 times the risk of gambling online compared with the lowest decile, and about 1.69 times among rural and suburban residents. The authors read this as partial substitution between the two channels rather than displacement, which matters for anyone arguing that closing shops reduces harm.

### [Exploring the relationships between problem and responsible gambling behaviors across time](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.addbeh.2026.108873)

*Addictive Behaviors, United States.*

A five-wave panel of 2,942 adult gamblers followed for about two years with the Positive Play Scale and the PGSI. Positive Play scores rose and problem-gambling symptoms fell over the period, and increases in gambling literacy specifically were associated with larger reductions in severity; the other Positive Play domains were not. The panel was not a trial, so the direction of the literacy effect is open, but it is a reasonable target for prevention messaging to test.

## Policy and public health news

- [Will SCOTUS take up prediction markets case after CFTC issues proposed rules on swaps?](https://igamingbusiness.com/prediction-markets/cftc-designates-sports-contracts-as-swaps-proposed-rules/) On October 9 the CFTC proposed defining swaps to include event contracts on sports, politics, culture and weather, with an interim rule excluding casino-style games; the Third, Sixth and Ninth Circuits have now split on the question.
- [Underdog sues Tennessee after state threatens fantasy license](https://sbcamericas.com/2026/10/15/underdog-sues-tn-prediction-markets/) The state's cease-and-desist letter tied Underdog's fantasy license to its event contracts; it is the company's seventh suit against a state.
- [Massachusetts Regulators Ask Operators If AI Targets Losing Bettors](https://www.legalsportsreport.com/281888/massachusetts-regulators-ask-operators-if-ai-targets-losing-bettors/) More than 70 questions to every licensed sportsbook, including whether a patron's losses feed promotional targeting; responses are due November 3.
- [New York, Massachusetts targeting AI use by sportsbooks after DraftKings stories](https://sbcamericas.com/2026/10/15/states-ai-use-sportsbooks-draftkings/) New York's commission advanced proposed rules that would bar AI-personalized promotions without consent and require operators to identify and contact at-risk players.
- [Underspent Problem Gambling Funds Are a Warning Sign](https://www.ncpgambling.org/news/underspent-problem-gambling-funds-are-a-warning-sign/) NCPG's board president on the NAADGS budget report: states allocated about $188 million for problem-gambling services in FY2025 and spent about $145 million, with workforce shortages the main reason.
- [NBA, WNBA Launching Responsible Gaming Campaign With NCPG](https://www.legalsportsreport.com/281846/nba-wnba-launching-responsible-gaming-campaign-with-ncpg/) The campaign, debuting with the new season, names prediction markets alongside sportsbooks.
- [Poll: Most Americans Oppose Betting On Elections Through Prediction Markets](https://www.legalsportsreport.com/281709/poll-most-americans-oppose-betting-on-elections-through-prediction-markets/) Reuters/Ipsos, 4,506 adults: three-quarters consider event contracts gambling, and opposition to election betting crosses party lines.
- [Colorado: UNLV study finds high problem gambling rate among sports bettors](https://cdcgaming.com/colorado-unlv-study-finds-high-problem-gambling-rate-among-sports-bettors/) Interim results from a 500-person state sample; the full report follows the Super Bowl.
